With all the madness around I am pondering how we got here. As you get older things get madder. Is it me or is it them? You be the judge.

<!--more-->
<br>
I can’t help wondering what it takes not to lose your mind. And no, I’m not talking about mental health, “wow is me” therapy-speak. I mean the everyday sanity that stops you from either taking revenge on the world or moving into a cave and starting counting rocks. The kind of normal madness we all carry around. It’s just a question of personality and your ability to deal with life. In the extreme, if you’re really good at it, it might even lead to happiness. But let’s start with the modest goal of not going completely mad.<br><br>

## Living on your own
I’ve noticed that people who live on their own tend to turn a bit funny after a while. Often angry. Often developing very strange ideas about things. Not necessarily full-blown conspiracy theorists, but still holding strong opinions that contradict common sense and basic reality.<br>

It’s odd, really. My father could never understand why I lived in share houses. “Why don’t you get your own place?” he kept saying, and I kept ignoring. So other people clearly find it desirable. Do they know it will slowly send them mad?<br><br>

## Living in isolated environments
Ever been to the country? If yes, you know what I mean.<br><br>

## Not working
Yes, in the traditional sense: “going to work” or doing useful (maybe) stuff for money. Not working will make you go funny. Surprising, really, because we all wish we didn’t have to go to work, without realising the danger of the alternative.<br>

I’ve met numerous examples. You’d think work is the thing that drives you crazy. The stress, the fear, the endless mulling over stupid work things that really don’t matter all that much. <br>I’m close to that scary point called retirement, fully aware of the danger that lies on the other side. I’ll have to cross that precipice at some point.<br><br>

## Trouble
Yeah, things always go wrong. House stuff, car stuff, children stuff, neighbours… everything, all the bloody time. You wish it didn’t. You wish for tranquility, where everything just works and nobody hassles you.<br>

And yet! Humans excel under adversity. If you don’t have constant trouble to deal with, you’ll go mad. Counter-intuitive, really, but true. Just look at the rich and famous. It’s almost impossible for them not to lose their minds.<br><br>

## So what to do?
Deal with it and pour yourself a big scotch at the end of the day. Light your pipe, sink into the big armchair, and pretend you’re in a period drama.<br>

But wait—you shouldn’t do that either. It’s bad for your brain, say the doctors. They suspect it causes dementia.<br>

Oh, is there no way of winning? So your choices are: stress out, don’t sleep, and go mad—or relax, take drugs, and get demented. <br>Maybe do a lot of yoga and drink water. Maybe. But probably not for me.<br>

Actually, if my anecdotal experience is anything to go by, all my dear dead friends so far were very healthy-living types. It’s all the abusers who are still around, so I’m not sure that “healthy lifestyle” thing is working either. What good is it if, on average, for every ten thousand people it works out for the better, but you’re the one dying of cancer after all that exercising and drinking water?<br><br>

Just get stressed out, get drunk, and die of a heart attack. That’s the way it’s meant to work.<br><br>
